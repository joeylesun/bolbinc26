Water application

Disinfection at the point of flow.

A compact UV-C LED reactor can treat water as it passes through a controlled optical chamber. The system is designed around absorption, flow, path length, mixing, and the dose required for the target organism.

View draft parameters →Discuss this application
Application story

The reactor is part of the optical design.

This draft explains how Bolb’s UV-C LED platform could fit into the complete product architecture. Final language should be supported by application-specific test data.

01 — The challenge

The challenge

Water quality can change UV transmission dramatically. Flow channels may also create fast paths that receive less exposure than the average volume.

02 — The UV-C role

The UV-C role

LEDs are arranged around or along a reactor to illuminate the active water volume. Reflective materials and mixing features can increase the probability that each fluid element receives the required dose.

03 — The system story

The system story

The development process links UV transmittance, flow rate, reactor geometry, thermal management, and microbiological targets. Performance is then verified at the defined operating envelope—not only at a single laboratory point.

Development path

From requirement to validated system.

1

Characterize the water

Measure UV transmittance or absorption, temperature, flow range, fouling risk, and target organism.

2

Design the reactor

Select wavelength, emitter count, optical path, reflector, window material, and hydraulic geometry.

3

Test the dose response

Validate reduction across minimum and maximum flow, end-of-life output, and realistic water quality.
