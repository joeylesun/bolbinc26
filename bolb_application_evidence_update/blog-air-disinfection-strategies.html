Air application guide

Upper-room or targeted treatment: choosing an air-disinfection strategy.

The two approaches use the same core solid-state UV-C technology but solve different occupancy, dose-delivery, and safety problems.

Draft technical article·Approx. 6-minute read·Return to air solutions

Air disinfection is not one single engineering category. A continuously occupied classroom and an intermittently treated patient room may both need additional microbial control, but they require very different optical fields and operating logic. Bolb’s current air portfolio reflects that distinction with an upper-room fixture and a targeted air-and-surface fixture.

Upper-room air: continuous treatment above occupants

Upper-room UVGI creates a treatment zone near the ceiling. Air movement—created by HVAC, fans, thermal plumes, and normal room circulation—carries contaminated air upward and through the irradiated zone. The strategy is intended for occupied spaces because the optical design limits UV-C below the specified height.

The main design challenge

The fixture must deliver useful irradiance in the upper room without allowing unsafe exposure in the occupied zone. Ceiling height, room dimensions, air mixing, fixture spacing, surface reflectance, and existing ventilation all affect the result. A successful design should therefore be evaluated as part of the whole room, not as a light source in isolation.

Product architectureThe AdaptUV Upper Room fixture uses four S3535-H LEDs at 265 nm, with 600 mW total UV-C output and a shielded lateral distribution pattern. The data sheet specifies installation at 9 ft or higher, less than 0.1 µW/cm² below 6 ft, 11 W maximum power consumption, and a rated lifetime above 15,000 hours.

This approach is a strong fit when the goal is ongoing room-air treatment in patient-care spaces, senior-living facilities, classrooms, offices, restaurants, and other occupied environments. It is designed to supplement—not replace—ventilation, filtration, and housekeeping practices.

Targeted air & surface: controlled cycles for local risk

Targeted treatment concentrates UV-C in a defined downward zone. This enables a stronger local dose for room air and exposed surfaces, but it also makes occupancy detection and optical shutdown essential. The product therefore combines the light source with sensors, an iris, visual status indicators, and cycle logic.

The main design challenge

Distance and line-of-sight dominate surface treatment. Objects can cast shadows, reflective materials can change the optical field, and people or pets may enter unexpectedly. The treatment area, mounting height, cycle duration, and room layout must be considered together.

Product architectureThe AdaptUV Targeted Air & Surface fixture uses 265 nm UV-C LEDs with 900 mW typical radiometric power and 50 µW/cm² irradiance at 1 m. It combines 2.4 GHz radar and PIR sensing with an automatic iris, a 120° detection field of view, an 80° light cone, and a maximum stated treatment area of 15 m² per unit.

This strategy is suited to healthcare rooms, reception spaces, restrooms, hotel rooms, ambulances, rideshare vehicles, and other environments where targeted cycles can be scheduled around access. Calculated examples in the data sheet illustrate how treatment time changes substantially with organism susceptibility and surface conditions.

A practical selection framework

  • Choose upper-room treatment when continuous operation in an occupied room is the primary objective and the ceiling height and room geometry support a shielded upper-zone installation.
  • Choose targeted treatment when the goal includes exposed surfaces or a local high-dose zone and occupancy can be controlled through sensors and cycle logic.
  • Evaluate both as complete systems. Room airflow, fixture placement, optical measurements, occupancy patterns, microbial targets, and validation methods matter more than a single source specification.
