Food-safety application engineering

Place UV-C at the right intervention point.

Food applications may treat process water, exposed product surfaces, packaging, equipment, storage environments, or transport. Source geometry must follow line speed, product presentation, shadowing, moisture, temperature, and quality constraints.

Explore intervention points →View component options
How to read this application page

Bolb supplies the UV-C source. The complete systems shown are real-world implementation references.

A microbial-reduction result belongs to the complete system and its test conditions—not to an LED by itself. Bolb LEDs and modules provide wavelength and optical power; the OEM controls geometry, dose distribution, airflow or flow, shielding, sensing, thermal design, and final validation.

Bolb LED, module, array, integration support System builder Fixture, reactor, controls, certification Published claim Only for the tested organism and conditions

Validation inputs

Microbial reduction is only one acceptance criterion.

Design inputWhy it matters
Organism and target reductionDefines the dose basis and representative microbial test.
Line speed / exposure timeSets required irradiance and source length along the process path.
Product presentationShape, rotation, overlap, texture, and shadowing determine dose uniformity.
Material and product qualityEvaluate color, flavor, nutrients, polymers, seals, labels, and other UV-sensitive attributes.
EnvironmentMoisture, temperature, cleaning chemicals, dust, and washdown affect package and enclosure design.
Worker safetyShielding, access controls, interlocks, maintenance mode, and verification are required.

Do not publish a general food-safety efficacy claim without validation for the specific food, surface, organism, dose, line condition, and quality endpoint.
