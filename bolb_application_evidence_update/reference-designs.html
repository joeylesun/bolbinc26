Scope

Reduce uncertainty at the interfaces.

A reference architecture is a starting point for engineering—not a substitute for the customer’s system-level design controls, safety review, and application validation.

01 — Source and optics

Select wavelength, output, emitter count, and optical layout

Translate the dose target and working geometry into a packaged LED, standard module, or custom-array architecture.

02 — Electrical and thermal

Define current regulation and the heat path

Coordinate strings, drivers, monitoring, protection, MCPCB contact, heatsink, airflow, or liquid cooling.

03 — Safety and validation

Plan interlocks, measurement, and representative testing

Connect the light engine to shielding, access control, sensors, calibration, dose mapping, microbial studies, and lifecycle testing.

Typical engagement

From requirements to a measurable light engine.

1

Requirements review

Application, geometry, organism, reduction target, treatment time, flow or line speed, environment, and production goal.

2

Architecture and prototype

Emitter/module selection, optical layout, driver, thermal path, mechanical interface, and prototype measurement plan.

3

Verification and transfer

Irradiance maps, temperature data, source-life assumptions, safety tests, documentation, and production-design handoff.

Information to provide

A useful review starts with system constraints.

CategoryUseful inputs
ApplicationAir, water, surface, food, industrial, laboratory, or other treatment objective
Optical targetWavelength, irradiance, dose, treatment time, working distance, and uniformity
GeometryEnvelope, field shape, flow path, line width, reflective materials, and optical obstructions
Electrical / thermalAvailable power, driver interface, ambient, heatsink volume, airflow or coolant
Safety / controlsEnclosure, occupancy, access, interlocks, sensors, state logic, and maintenance mode
CommercialPrototype schedule, qualification plan, expected annual volume, and production location
