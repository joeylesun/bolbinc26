Surface application engineering

Scale the source and control architecture to the space.

Surface treatment is dominated by distance, line of sight, shadows, cycle time, material response, and human access. Bolb supplies the UV-C source; customers build the fixture and safety system.

How to read this application page

Bolb supplies the UV-C source. The complete systems shown are real-world implementation references.

A microbial-reduction result belongs to the complete system and its test conditions—not to an LED by itself. Bolb LEDs and modules provide wavelength and optical power; the OEM controls geometry, dose distribution, airflow or flow, shielding, sensing, thermal design, and final validation.

Scale 01 · Compact treatment zone

Concentrate dose on visible surfaces and local air.

A compact system can supervise one defined optical field, but it must still manage the lowest-dose point, shadows, access paths, clear-time logic, and the difference between calculated and measured dose.

The challenge

Cover the intended surfaces without exposing people.

Bed rails, medical carts, desks, counters, equipment, and other surfaces sit at different distances and angles and may be shadowed by objects or room geometry.

How Bolb components fit

Packaged LEDs or a compact custom array.

Select emitter count, beam, board layout, cooling, and working distance from the dose map. Use the system’s sensing and iris architecture to control access—not as a substitute for optical validation.

Scale 02 · Large coordinated space

Control many treatment zones independently.

Warehouses, shopping malls, airports, and large lobbies require more than a larger lamp. The source system, cameras, mapped zones, safety logic, communications, and event records must operate as a coordinated architecture.

The challenge

Keep treatment productive as people move across a large floor plan.

One occupied zone should not necessarily disable every fixture, but each active source must respond safely to people entering its treatment field or to loss of the relevant sensing channel.

How Bolb components fit

Modules and custom arrays become independently controlled light engines.

Each fixture can use a standard or custom Bolb source format selected for mounting height, treatment radius, dose target, cooling, and electrical interface.

Control-architecture example · No microbial efficacy claim

YOLO-based multi-camera UV-C coordination.

The SUVOS development software illustrates how a system builder can coordinate many UV-C fixtures with camera calibration, YOLO human detection, mapped floor zones, independent WAIT/CLEAN/STANDBY logic, a local dashboard, and event logging. This software layer manages access and fixture state; it does not by itself determine microbial reduction.

Application evidence

Separate optical efficacy from occupancy-control capability.

The compact fixture data sheet provides calculated surface examples. The large-space software provides a control architecture, not a kill-rate result.

Customer calculation

90% E. coli reduction in 3.1 minutes

Calculated for a 36 ft² surface field at the stated 3 ft counter geometry.

Useful message
Compact UV-C LED fixtures can deliver short-cycle treatment where distance and line of sight are controlled
Required confirmation
Measure the lowest-dose point and perform a representative microbial test in the final room
Customer calculation

90% spore reduction in 64 minutes

Calculated over the same 36 ft² field using a substantially higher dose requirement.

What it demonstrates
Cycle time can change dramatically with organism susceptibility
Why it matters
A single “surface kill rate” is scientifically misleading without organism and dose conditions
Control proof, not efficacy proof

SUVOS enables zone-specific operation

Clear zones may continue their configured cycles while an occupied zone returns to WAIT.

Potential benefit
More usable treatment time in a large facility
No numerical claim yet
Reduction still depends on each fixture’s dose and the validated treatment cycle
Correct positioning

Describe SUVOS as an occupancy-aware control example and the compact fixture as a dose-delivery example. Do not combine them into one unsupported large-space disinfection percentage.
