Reference designs

Reference designs

Reference designs connect UV-C components to real system architectures. They can give OEM teams a starting point for optical layout, drive electronics, thermal design, controls, and validation planning.

PRODUCT FAMILYReference designs
Product story

A faster path from promising component to testable system.

This draft positions reference designs as engineering accelerators. They are not finished products or substitutes for application-specific safety, regulatory, and microbial validation.

01 — Architecture

Start from a known system pattern

Reference concepts can demonstrate how emitters, arrays, optics, drivers, thermal paths, sensors, and enclosures fit together.

02 — Adaptation

Modify around the customer requirement

Geometry, flow, line speed, materials, power budget, controls, and manufacturing constraints determine what must change.

03 — Validation

Use the prototype to close the engineering loop

Optical, thermal, electrical, safety, reliability, and microbial testing confirm whether the adapted design meets the actual requirement.

Integration path

From reference architecture to customer prototype.

1

Select the closest architecture

Choose an air, water, surface, or food-processing concept that resembles the operating environment.

2

Customize the critical interfaces

Adapt source quantity, mechanics, optics, driver, thermal solution, sensors, controls, and service strategy.

3

Test against the real requirement

Validate dose, microbial performance, temperature, electrical behavior, safety logic, and reliability.

Engineering considerations

The interfaces a useful reference design should make explicit.

◈
Optical architecture

Source placement, reflector or lens strategy, working distance, and expected dose field.

↯
Electrical system

Driver topology, power input, control interface, monitoring, and protection.

△
Thermal system

Heat path, heatsink, airflow or liquid cooling, sensors, and operating limits.

✓
Validation plan

Measurements, microbial testing, safety review, materials, and representative use conditions.
